# Core Processes Matrix

Team 6, Airline CRM, Lab 4 (Exercise 3). Contributor: Rizwanul Abidin Asim (b00099449).

The matrix describes the six major processes of the Level 0 DFD. For each process it lists the data flows in and out, as drawn in the Level 0 DFD, and the rules that turn the input data into output data.

| Process | Input data | Output data | Rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 Manage Customer Profiles | Search criteria and profile details (from the CSR)<br>Reservation records, imported (from the Reservation System)<br>Profiles and bookings retrieved (from D1) | Booking history (to the CSR)<br>Profiles and imported bookings saved (to D1) | A search must use at least one identifier, which is the name, customer ID, email, phone number or booking reference. When more than one profile matches, all matches are listed and the CSR selects one.<br>Before contact details are changed, the identity of the customer is checked against the stored date of birth and a recent booking reference.<br>A profile cannot be saved without a name and at least one contact method. An email or phone number already used by another profile is rejected, which prevents duplicate records.<br>If the search finds no match, a new profile is created with the confirmation of the CSR.<br>Each imported reservation record is validated and matched to a customer before it is saved as a booking. A record that fails validation is marked as rejected with its error and is not saved as a booking.<br>Booking and travel history is read only and comes from the imported reservation data. If an imported record is incomplete or malformed, only that record is left out and a notice is shown, and the rest of the history still loads.<br>Every profile creation or change is saved with the staff ID and a timestamp for the audit trail. |
| 2.0 Manage Customer Service | Request details, status updates, feedback and loyalty adjustments (from the CSR)<br>Balance and tier (read from D1)<br>Requests retrieved (from D2) | Confirmations, loyalty status and notification alerts (to the CSR)<br>Adjusted points (written to D1)<br>Requests saved (to D2) | A service request can only be logged against an existing customer profile.<br>Category and priority are required. The status only moves forward from Open to In Progress to Resolved to Closed, so a request cannot be closed before it has been worked on.<br>A resolution note is required before a request can be closed, and every status or assignment change is written to the audit trail with the staff ID and the previous and new values.<br>Each feedback entry carries a satisfaction rating and a category and is linked to a specific booking or interaction, so that satisfaction trends can be measured.<br>A manual loyalty adjustment is rejected unless it has both a reason code and a note. Only staff granted the point adjustment permission may make one, and an adjustment above 3,000 points is held for review by Airline Management before it takes effect.<br>After an adjustment the membership tier is recalculated, and any tier change is written to the tier history with its date and reason. |
| 3.0 Manage B2B Accounts | Account, agreement and interaction details (from the Account Manager)<br>B2B account records retrieved (from D3) | Account records and confirmations (to the Account Manager)<br>B2B account records saved (to D3) | Only the assigned account manager can create or amend a corporate or partner account.<br>Organisation name, agreement start and end dates, negotiated terms and the assigned account manager are mandatory, and a record missing any of them is rejected.<br>The agreement end date must fall after the start date. Once the end date has passed, the account is flagged as expired and no longer appears in views of active accounts until it is renewed.<br>Each record is either a corporate client or a partner or vendor, such as a catering or maintenance provider. The type decides which fields apply, since a vendor has no negotiated fare terms.<br>Interaction entries can only be added. Each stores the date, staff member and summary and cannot be edited or deleted afterwards, so the engagement history stays reliable. |
| 4.0 Manage Campaigns and Segments | Segment criteria and campaign details (from Marketing Staff)<br>Tier, route and travel history (from D1)<br>Complaints history (from D2)<br>Saved segment retrieved (from D4) | Match count and campaign summary (to Marketing Staff)<br>Segments and campaigns saved (to D4) | A segment must use at least one criterion from loyalty tier, route, number of flights within a stated period, or complaint history. When several criteria are given, a customer must satisfy all of them.<br>The number of matching customers is calculated and shown before the segment can be saved, and a segment that matches no customers cannot be saved.<br>A campaign must target a saved segment, read back from D4, and must have a start and end date.<br>Customers who have opted out of marketing, who are listed in a do not call registry, or who have an unresolved complaint are excluded even when they match the criteria.<br>Campaign results count a booking only when a targeted customer books the promoted route within the offer period, and each such booking is counted once per campaign (FR-12). |
| 5.0 Administer Users and Access | Instructions to create or edit staff accounts and permissions (from the CRM Administrator)<br>Audit log (read from D3) | Confirmations, access decisions and audit report (to the CRM Administrator)<br>Accounts and roles saved (to D3) | Only the CRM administrator may create, modify or deactivate a staff account or change its permissions.<br>Each account has exactly one of the five defined roles, and that role decides which actions the account may perform.<br>Every restricted action is checked against the role of the user on the server before it is carried out, not only hidden on screen. A request the role does not permit is refused, and the attempt is written to the audit log with the user ID, action and timestamp.<br>Passwords are stored only as hashes that cannot be reversed, so they can be checked but never read back, even by someone holding a copy of the database.<br>Accounts are deactivated rather than deleted, so existing audit entries still refer to a valid user. Audit entries can only be added and are kept for at least 12 months. |
| 6.0 Generate Reports | Date range, period and export format (from Airline Management)<br>Loyalty and satisfaction data (from D2)<br>Partner engagement and records (from D3) | Trends, dashboards and exported reports (to Airline Management) | A date range must be given. The end date cannot be earlier than the start date, and the start date cannot be in the future.<br>Reports only read data, so generating or exporting a report never changes the records behind it.<br>Average satisfaction is calculated for each category, and any category whose average falls below the threshold (3.00/5.00 by default) is flagged for review.<br>A report can be exported as PDF or CSV and reproduces exactly the figures shown on screen. Only roles permitted to view reports may generate or export them. |

## Changes from the Lab 4 submission

The matrix was aligned with later labs during the final consistency check:

- Process names and data flow names now match the Level 0 DFD exactly (for example 2.0 Manage Customer Service, and Requests saved to D2), including the Reservation System import into 1.0 and the D4 Marketing Data store used by 4.0 that were added to the DFD after the instructor's feedback.
- The identity check uses the date of birth and a recent booking reference, as in scenario S-01 and `verifyIdentity(customerId, dob, bookingRef)`. The membership ID was removed.
- The notification to the customer after a tier change was removed, because the Lab 6 consistency check removed it from UC-09 as unsupported by FR-14.
- Passwords are described as one-way hashes rather than encryption, matching NFR-12.
